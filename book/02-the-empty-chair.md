# Story 2: The Empty Chair

*Feeling: grief and loss. Name of Allah: Ar-Rahman, The Most Merciful. Example: Sayyida Zaynab, peace be upon her, after Karbala.*

---

Nobody sat in Jiddo's chair any more.

It was still by the window, with the cushion that had gone flat in the shape of him. His glasses were still on the little table. But Jiddo had gone back to Allah three weeks ago, and now the chair was just a chair.

Noor didn't like looking at it. She didn't like not looking at it either.

At dinner, Baba said, "Eat, Noor." But her throat felt too small for food.

That night, a golden light blinked on beside her pillow.

"Hello, Noor," said Fanoos.

"I don't want to go to the garden," said Noor. "I don't want to feel better. If I feel better it means I forgot him."

Fanoos was quiet for a moment.

"Feeling better doesn't mean forgetting," the firefly said. "But we don't have to feel better tonight. We can just go and sit with someone who understands. Would that be all right?"

Noor thought about it. Then she nodded.

---

The Lantern Garden was dim tonight. Half the lanterns were unlit.

By a pond sat a big grey goose, alone. The water was very still, and in it Noor could see the reflection of two geese, even though there was only one.

"Who is that in the water?" Noor asked.

"My sister," said the goose. "We flew together every year, my whole life. This spring she got tired and lay down in the reeds and didn't get up. Now when I look in the water I still see her. And when I look up, she isn't there."

Noor sat down beside the goose. She didn't say "It will be all right." She didn't say "Don't be sad." She just sat.

After a while she said, "My Jiddo went back to Allah. His chair is empty. Everyone keeps saying he's in a better place, and I know that's true, but I still want him in his chair."

"Yes," said the goose. "That's exactly it."

They watched the water together.

"Do you know about Sayyida Zaynab?" Noor said at last. "Peace be upon her. She was the granddaughter of the Prophet. In one day, at Karbala, she lost her brother Imam Husayn, and her other brothers, and her own sons, and almost everyone she loved. In one day."

"How did she keep standing?" asked the goose.

"She cried," said Noor. "She cried a lot. Crying wasn't weak. Imam Husayn's son, Imam Zayn al-Abidin, cried for his father for years and years, and he was an Imam. But Sayyida Zaynab also said something. When a cruel man asked her what she thought of what had happened, she said she saw nothing but beauty. She meant that even in the worst day, she could see that Allah was there, and that her family had gone to Him, and He is Ar-Rahman, The Most Merciful. He is more kind to them than anyone ever was."

"That is a good thing to know," said the goose. "But it doesn't stop the missing."

"No," said Noor. "I think the missing is love that doesn't have anywhere to go."

"Then where should I put it?" asked the goose.

Fanoos glowed softly. "You could send it after her," the firefly said. "That's what dua is. It's love with an address."

So Noor and the goose closed their eyes, and Noor said the words Baba said at the graveyard: "O Allah, forgive him and have mercy on him, and put him with Muhammad and the family of Muhammad." And the goose said something in goose that meant the same thing.

When Noor opened her eyes, one more lantern in the garden had lit.

"Is she happy?" the goose asked. "My sister?"

"I don't know everything," said Noor honestly. "But I know Ar-Rahman is with her. And you can send her love every single day, as much as you want. It never runs out."

---

Back in her room, Noor got out of bed. She walked down the hall in the dark. She went to Jiddo's chair, and she sat in it, in the dent shaped like him.

She cried, quite a lot. It was all right. Nobody said stop. Sayyida Zaynab cried too.

Then she said, "O Allah, forgive him and have mercy on him," and she went back to bed.

In the morning, she ate breakfast. The chair was still empty. But it didn't feel like a hole any more. It felt like a place to send love from.

## Questions for you

1. Have you ever missed someone so much it hurt somewhere in your body? Where?
2. Noor was afraid that feeling better meant forgetting. Do you think that's true?
3. Sayyida Zaynab cried, and she also kept standing. Can a person do both?
4. Noor said missing is "love that doesn't have anywhere to go." Where can you send it?
5. What is one thing you remember about someone you miss? Tell it out loud.

## Something to try

**Send the love.** Think of the person you miss. Say their name. Say one thing you loved about them. Then say a dua for them: "O Allah, forgive them and have mercy on them." You can do this every night. It never runs out.

## A dua to remember

**إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّا إِلَيْهِ رَاجِعُونَ**

*Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un.*

"We belong to Allah, and to Him we return." (Quran 2:156)

## For grown-ups

Children grieve in bursts. They may cry hard and then ask to play five minutes later, and that is normal, not shallow. The most common mistakes adults make are rushing a child to feel better and hiding their own tears. Our tradition does not ask us to hide grief: the weeping of Imam Zayn al-Abidin for his father is well known *(verify)*, and the words of Sayyida Zaynab in the court of Ibn Ziyad, "I saw nothing but beauty", are widely reported *(verify the wording and source with your scholar)*. Tell the truth in plain words: the person died, they will not come back in this life, and it is nobody's fault. Avoid saying the person "went to sleep" or "went away", which can make children afraid of sleep or of people leaving. Give them a job: a dua to say, a photo to keep, a story to tell. If a child stops eating or playing for more than a couple of weeks, blames themselves, or says they want to join the person, seek help promptly.
