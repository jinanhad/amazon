# Story 5: Too Small to Help

*Feeling: helplessness. Name of Allah: Al-Karim, The Generous. Example: Sayyida Fatima, peace be upon her, giving her food to the poor.*

---

There was a family sleeping in the school hall.

Noor saw them when she went to fetch her jacket. A mother, a father, three children and a grandmother, on mattresses on the floor, with their bags in a pile. Their house was gone. The teacher said so, quietly, to another teacher.

Noor stood in the doorway for a long time. Then she went home and she didn't say anything at dinner.

That night the golden light blinked on.

"You've gone very quiet," said Fanoos.

"There's a family with no house," said Noor. "And I'm eight. I can't build a house. I can't do anything. I'm too small."

"That is a heavy thing for a small person to carry," said Fanoos. "Come and meet the smallest creature in the garden."

---

By the roots of a great tree, Noor had to crouch and then lie flat on her tummy to see. It was an ant.

The ant was standing beside a leaf that had fallen across the ants' path. The leaf was enormous. To the ant it was a mountain range.

"I can't move it," said the ant. "The others are all on the far side and they can't get home. And I'm one ant. What's one ant?"

"I know," said Noor. "I'm one girl."

They looked at the leaf together.

"Do you know about Sayyida Fatima?" Noor said after a while. "Peace be upon her. She was the daughter of the Prophet. One time, she and Imam Ali and their children had been fasting for three days, and they had almost no food. Just enough bread for the family. And a poor person knocked at the door and asked for food. And they gave it. All of it. And the next day another person knocked, and they gave again. And the next day, again. They stayed hungry, and they gave the bread."

"Why?" said the ant. "It was such a small amount of bread. It wouldn't fix being poor."

"That's what I thought too," said Noor. "But Allah put it in the Quran. He liked it so much that He wrote it down forever. It wasn't about fixing being poor. It was about the giving. Allah is Al-Karim, The Generous. When we give, even a tiny bit, we're doing the thing that is most like Him."

"But I can't give a leaf," said the ant.

"No," said Fanoos. "But can you give a push?"

The ant looked at the leaf. It put its front legs on the edge. It pushed.

The leaf did not move.

"See?" said the ant.

"Push again," said Noor.

The ant pushed again. The leaf did not move. But one very small part of the edge lifted, and underneath it, from the far side, a second ant squeezed through the gap.

"You came!" said the second ant. "We saw the leaf move. We thought someone was there."

Now there were two ants pushing. Then a third came through the gap. Then ten. Then Noor lost count. And the leaf did not fly away, but it slid, a little, and then a little more, until the path was open.

"I didn't move the leaf," said the first ant, out of breath.

"No," said Noor. "You moved the edge. That's what the others needed to see."

---

In the morning, Noor asked Mama if she could take something to school. Mama found a blanket that was clean, and a tin of biscuits, and Noor put her own best pencil in too, the one with the eraser shaped like a strawberry.

She walked into the hall and gave it to the smallest child, who was about Adam's age.

"It's not a house," Noor said, a bit embarrassed.

The child looked at the strawberry eraser for a long time. Then she smiled.

Later that week, the teacher said that other children had brought things too. Nobody had told them to. They had just seen Noor.

---

## Questions for you

1. Have you ever wanted to help someone and felt too small?
2. The ant couldn't move the leaf. What could it do?
3. Sayyida Fatima gave away bread when her family was hungry. What do you think that felt like?
4. What is one small thing you could give or do this week? Not a big thing. A small thing.
5. Why do you think Allah wrote such a small act in the Quran?

## Something to try

**One small push.** Pick one thing that is bothering you about the world, big or small. Now pick the smallest thing you could do about it that takes less than ten minutes. A drawing for someone sad. A coin in the box. Carrying a bag. Do it today. Notice what happens next.

## For grown-ups

Helplessness in children often looks like withdrawal, going quiet, or a sudden lack of interest, especially after they see suffering they cannot fix. What restores a child is not being told it is not their problem. It is being given something real and small to do. Agency is the cure for helplessness. The story of the Ahl al-Bayt giving their food to a poor person, an orphan and a captive on three consecutive days is the tradition linked in Shia commentary to the verses of Surah al-Insan, 76:8 and 76:9 *(verify the narration and its details with your scholar)*. If a child's withdrawal lasts more than a couple of weeks, or they say things like "nothing matters", seek help.
