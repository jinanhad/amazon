# Story 6: The New Room

*Feeling: missing home after moving. Name of Allah: Al-Qarib, The Near. Example: the family of Imam Husayn, peace be upon them, far from home.*

---

The new room had the wrong ceiling.

Noor lay on the mattress and looked at it. At home the ceiling had a crack shaped like a running dog. This ceiling had nothing. It was just white. The window was on the wrong side. The sounds outside were the wrong sounds. Even the dark was a different dark.

They had left in a hurry. Baba said it was safer here, at Auntie's. Baba said it was only for a while. But Noor's bed was there, and her books were there, and the running-dog crack was there, and she was here.

She turned to the wall so nobody would see her face.

A small golden light blinked on, on the wrong side of the room.

"Even you're in the wrong place," Noor whispered.

"I'm exactly where you are," said Fanoos. "That's the only place I'm ever in. Come on."

---

The Lantern Garden was different tonight. Noor came in through the gate and stopped. The trees were the same, but the paths had moved. The pond was on the left instead of the right.

"It changed," she said.

"Gardens do," said Fanoos. "Look who's here."

On a rock sat a small tortoise. It was very still, and it had its head half inside its shell.

"Hello," said Noor.

"Hello," said the tortoise, without coming out.

"What's wrong?"

"They moved the garden," said the tortoise. "My rock was over there. Now it's here. Nothing is where it should be. I want to go back to before."

Noor sat down beside the rock.

"We moved," she said. "Not the garden. Us. My whole family. I have a new room and it's got the wrong ceiling."

The tortoise came out a little. "How do you sleep with the wrong ceiling?"

"I don't know yet," said Noor honestly.

They sat.

"Do you know," said Noor slowly, "about Sayyida Zaynab and the children of Imam Husayn? Peace be upon them. After Karbala, they were taken away from their home. A long, long way. To a city called Kufa and then a city called Sham. They didn't choose to go. They walked and rode for days, and they slept in strange places, and nothing was where it should be. And they were the family of the Prophet."

"How did they bear it?" said the tortoise.

"My grandmother says that Sayyida Zaynab prayed every night on the road. Every single night, even the worst ones. Even when she was so tired she prayed sitting down. Because wherever they took her, the prayer was the same. And Allah was the same. She couldn't carry her house. But she carried her prayer, and Allah was in it, and He is Al-Qarib, The Near. The Quran says He is with you wherever you are."

"Wherever?" said the tortoise.

"Wherever."

The tortoise came all the way out of its shell. It looked at its rock.

"It's still my rock," it said. "It's just in a different place. And I'm still me. And Allah is still..." It stopped. "Al-Qarib?"

"Al-Qarib," said Noor.

"I have an idea," said Fanoos. "Noor, what did you bring from home?"

Noor thought. "Nothing. We left fast. Only... my prayer mat. Mama grabbed it."

"Then you brought home," said Fanoos.

---

In the new room, Noor got up. She found the bag. She found the prayer mat, rolled up tight, with the smell of the old house still in it.

She unrolled it on the floor of the wrong room, facing the way Baba had shown her. She sat on it.

And it was the same mat. And the qibla was the same qibla. And Allah was the same Allah, and He was Al-Qarib, here, in this room, exactly as much as in the old one.

She said, "*Wa huwa ma'akum ayna ma kuntum.* He is with you wherever you are."

Then she lay down on the mattress and looked at the plain white ceiling.

"Tomorrow," she said to it, "I'm going to find a crack in you shaped like something."

She fell asleep before she decided what.

## Questions for you

1. Have you ever had to sleep somewhere that wasn't home? What was the wrong thing about it?
2. What is one thing you would carry with you if you had to leave fast?
3. Sayyida Zaynab couldn't carry her house. What could she carry?
4. Is a place home because of the walls, or because of something else? What?
5. Where is Allah when you are somewhere new?

## Something to try

**Home in a bag.** Choose three small things that mean home to you: a prayer mat, a photo, a toy, a smell, a book. Put them together in one place. Wherever you are, when you take them out and lay them down, that spot is a little bit home. Do it tonight, even if you haven't moved.

## A dua to remember

**وَهُوَ مَعَكُمْ أَيْنَ مَا كُنتُمْ**

*Wa huwa ma'akum ayna ma kuntum.*

"And He is with you wherever you are." (Quran 57:4)

## For grown-ups

Displacement, even a short move to a relative's house, unsettles children more than adults expect, because children's sense of safety is tied to sameness: the same bed, the same route, the same sounds. What helps is not pretending the new place is home, but rebuilding small rituals fast: the same bedtime order, the same prayer spot, one familiar object in view. The account of Sayyida Zaynab praying every night on the journey after Karbala, including the night prayer prayed sitting from exhaustion, is widely related in Shia tradition *(verify the source with your scholar)*. Keep the Karbala details gentle at this age; the point is that she carried her prayer, not the suffering. If a child who has moved after a frightening event cannot sleep, wets the bed again, becomes clingy for weeks, or startles at sounds, seek help; these are normal early reactions but should ease over time.
