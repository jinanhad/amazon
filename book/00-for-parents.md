# A note for parents and carers

This is a book to read together, one story at a time, at bedtime or whenever a feeling is big. Each story is about ten minutes aloud.

## How to use it

1. **Read the story** without stopping to explain. Let the child sit inside it.
2. **Ask the questions** at the end. There are no right answers. If your child says "I don't know", that is an answer too. Try sharing your own answer first; children open up when they hear a grown-up admit to a feeling.
3. **Try the practice** together, once, right then. Children remember what they did more than what they heard.
4. **Say the dua** together. The Arabic is given with a simple transliteration and meaning. Saying it is more important than saying it perfectly.
5. **Read the grown-up note** on your own. It says what the feeling usually looks like in a child and what helps.

You do not need to read the stories in order. If your child is afraid of loud noises tonight, go to story one. If they are missing someone, go to story two.

## What this book is and is not

This book helps a child name a feeling, feel less alone with it, and remember that Allah is near and that the Prophet and his family, peace be upon them, felt these feelings too and showed us what to do with them. That is a lot, and it is enough for most ordinary big feelings.

It is not a replacement for a doctor, a counsellor or a psychologist. Please seek help from a professional if your child:

- has nightmares or fears that go on for weeks and get in the way of sleep, school or play,
- stops eating, stops playing, or stops wanting to be with people,
- talks about wanting to disappear or not wanting to be alive,
- has lived through something frightening and seems stuck in it, jumping at sounds, replaying it, or going silent.

Seeking help is not a lack of trust in Allah. Trusting Allah and going to the doctor are the same act done with two hands.

## A word on the religious content

This book is written for Shia Muslim families. The Quran verses, the names of Allah, the duas and the stories of the Ahl al-Bayt were chosen because they are well known and short enough for a child. The duas come from the Quran and from the Shia tradition, including the tasbih of Sayyida Fatima and Dua al-Faraj. ## A word on the fantasy

The Lantern Garden is a place in Noor's imagination, like a dream. Fanoos the firefly is a friend she imagines. Nothing in the garden has power of its own. Every good thing that happens there comes from Allah, and the story says so. The Prophet and the Imams, peace be upon them, never appear as characters in the garden; Noor only remembers what she has been taught about them. If your family prefers to keep stories free of talking animals, you can tell your child plainly: "This is a pretend garden Noor imagines, to help her think." Children accept this easily.
