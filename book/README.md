# Noor and the Lantern Garden
## Eight bedtime stories that help Shia Muslim children through big feelings

**Audience:** children aged 6 to 10, read with a parent. Shia (Twelver) Muslim families, in English first, Arabic edition later.

**Promise:** each story follows Noor, an eight-year-old girl, and Fanoos, a small firefly who appears when she has a feeling too big to hold. Together they visit the Lantern Garden, where every creature carries a feeling of its own. Noor helps the creature, and in helping, remembers one of Allah's names, one example from the Prophet and his family (peace be upon them), and one small thing she can do herself. Each story ends with questions for the child, something to try, a short dua, and a note for grown-ups.

**Why this book, honestly:** Islamic children's books on feelings are a real niche with loyal buyers, and books written specifically for Shia families are far fewer than for the wider Muslim market, so the competition is lighter. Buyers are mostly parents in the UK, US, Canada, Australia, the Gulf, and the Lebanese, Iraqi and Iranian diaspora. It will not earn like romance fiction. It can sell steadily for years, mostly as a paperback, and a series of three or four builds an audience. Choose it because it matters to you.

## Verification before publishing (not optional)

1. **Religious review by a qualified Shia scholar.** Have a sheikh or sayyid with hawza training read every story, especially the duas, the Quran references, the stories about the Ahl al-Bayt, and the hadith. I used only Quran verses I am confident about, and stories about the Ahl al-Bayt that are widely taught, but wording of every dua and hadith is marked *(verify)* and must be checked against Shia sources such as al-Kafi, Nahj al-Balagha, al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya and Mafatih al-Jinan.
2. **Child psychology review.** The practices are simple, widely used calming and naming techniques. A child psychologist or school counsellor should read the grown-up notes. The book is support, not therapy, and the parents' note says so.
3. **AI disclosure.** These drafts were written by Claude from your brief. On KDP that is AI-generated text and must be declared unless you rewrite it substantially in your own words. Illustrations made with an AI image tool must also be declared.
4. **Illustrations.** A children's book without pictures does not sell. Plan on eight to sixteen illustrations. Faces of the Prophet and the Imams are never drawn; the stories are written so no illustration needs them. See the listing plan.
